User Guidelines

What is allowed here, what is not, and what happens when somebody crosses the line.

Last updated 9 November 2026.

These Guidelines are part of the User Terms. Each section below matches a reason you can pick when you report somebody, so if something here is broken there is always a way to tell us about it.

1. Be who you say you are

FoitiFinder has no chat and no profile browsing. The only thing anyone has to go on is your profile, so it has to be true.

  • Your photos must be of you, recent enough to be recognisable, and your face must be visible in at least one.
  • Do not use anybody else's photos, a celebrity's, a stock image, or an AI-generated person.
  • Do not pretend to be a specific real person. That is impersonation and we remove accounts for it.
  • Do not run an account for somebody else, or share one.
  • Your name can be whatever you go by. It does not have to match your ID. It should not be a slogan, an advert or a handle for another service.

Report as: Fake profile → not the person in the photos, or impersonation.

2. Nobody under 18

This is the rule we enforce hardest and the only one with no benefit of the doubt.

  • You must be 18 or over to be here at all.
  • Do not put photos of children on your profile, including your own relatives, and including group photos. There is no good reason for a child to appear on a dating profile.
  • Any sexual content involving a minor results in immediate removal and is referred to the authorities. There is nothing to discuss.

If a profile looks like it belongs to someone under 18 — anything at all, even a hunch — report it. We would far rather check a hundred wrong guesses than miss one.

Report as: Underage → may be under 18, or sexual content involving a minor.

3. Keep your profile decent

  • No nudity or sexually explicit photos. Underwear and swimwear in an ordinary context are fine; a profile that is a sex advert is not.
  • No hate. Nothing that attacks or demeans people for their race, ethnicity, nationality, religion, disability, gender, gender identity or sexual orientation. Hate symbols count as hate.
  • No violence or gore, and nothing designed to disturb people who scroll past it.
  • No glorifying self-harm, eating disorders or suicide.
  • No illegal content, and nothing promoting illegal activity or the sale of drugs or weapons.

Report as: Profile content → nudity or sexual content, or hateful, violent or disturbing content.

4. Do not use this place to sell anything

FoitiFinder is for meeting people. It is not a marketing channel.

  • No advertising, promotion, affiliate links, recruiting or driving traffic anywhere.
  • No asking for money, gifts, cards, crypto or bank details, in any framing, from anybody. This is the most common scam on every dating app, and if it happens to you, report it rather than reasoning with it.
  • No accounts that exist to grow another account's following.
  • No automation: bots, scripts and bulk-swiping tools are not allowed.

Report as: Fake profile → spam, scam or asking for money.

5. After you match

When the second confirmation lands you have each other's Instagram handle. From there the app cannot see anything, which makes this the part that depends entirely on how people behave.

  • A match is not consent to anything. It is permission to say hello. Somebody who stops replying has answered.
  • No unsolicited sexual messages or images. Not in the first message, not ever without being asked.
  • Do not pass on what you were given. Somebody's handle, photos, name or anything they told you is theirs. Do not post it, screenshot it into a group chat, or show it around.
  • Do not threaten, follow, or keep contacting somebody who has asked you to stop. Block them and leave it.
  • Non-consensual intimate images, and threatening to share images, are among the most serious things anybody can do here. Report them and we will act, and so can the police.

If you removed a match from your list, the other person still has the handle you shared. That is a limit of the app, not a thing you agreed to. You can still report them: Account settings → Matches you removed lists everybody you removed, by name, and reporting one of them from there works exactly as it does from a profile — the reasons below included.

That list holds names and nothing else: no photos, and no way back to a profile. Removing a match is meant to stop you seeing somebody, and a screen that handed the faces back would quietly undo it.

The one thing we cannot help with is somebody who has deleted their account. There is nobody left to act against and nothing of theirs for us to remove, so the report has nowhere to land.

Report as: Harassment or something that happened after we matched → abusive messages, threats or stalking, sexual assault, shared my information, or made me feel unsafe. This group appears only once you have matched with somebody, because these are things that cannot have happened to a stranger.

6. How reporting works

  1. Open the profile and choose Report. Pick the category, then the specific reason, then add anything you want us to know — the free text is usually what makes a report actionable, so use it if you can. For somebody whose match you removed, the profile is no longer reachable — go to Account settings → Matches you removed instead, where the only thing you can do is report one of them.
  2. The person is removed from your deck immediately and will not be shown to you again. Any match between you disappears from your list.
  3. They are never told who reported them, and the match disappearing from your list does not change anything on theirs — so reporting somebody does not tip them off.
  4. We review it. Urgent categories — anything involving a minor, sexual assault, or threats and stalking — go to the front of the queue.
  5. You will not normally hear the outcome. Whether we removed somebody's account is their business, and telling you would also tell somebody determined enough to ask.

If you only want to stop seeing a profile, you do not need to report it — "Not interested" in the same menu does that without filing anything.

Reporting somebody to punish them for passing on you, or to get rid of a rival, is a breach of these Guidelines in itself. We can see how many reports one account sends.

7. What we do about it

Not everything is the same, so not every consequence is the same:

  • Content removed — a photo or a line of bio that breaks a rule comes down.
  • A warning — for a first, minor, fixable thing.
  • Suspension — the account stops working while we look into it.
  • Permanent removal — and you may not come back on a new account.
  • Referral to the authorities — where there is a risk to somebody, or where the law requires it.

Anything involving a minor, a sexual offence, or a credible threat goes straight to the last two. Everything else is a judgement made by a person, not by a score.

If we acted on your account and you think we got it wrong, write to [[EMAIL]]. A human will read it.

8. Staying safe when you meet somebody

We verify that an email address exists at a Greek university. That is all it proves. We do not run background checks on anybody, and a verified account is not a vouched-for person.

  • Talk on Instagram before you meet. A video call beats any number of photos.
  • Meet somewhere public the first time, and get yourself there and back.
  • Tell a friend where you are going and who with. Share your live location with them if you can.
  • Keep your phone charged and keep your drink with you.
  • Do not send money, and do not send intimate images to somebody you have not met.
  • If something feels off, leave. You do not owe anybody an explanation.

In an emergency in Greece, call 112 (the European emergency number) or 100 for the police. For gender-based violence, the national helpline is 15900, free and staffed around the clock. We are an app with no chat and no night shift — these numbers reach people who can actually help you now.
